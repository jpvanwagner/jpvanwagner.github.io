Community Engagement

Partnering for success and building culture.

Cultural learning

In Alaska, I developed, taught, and shared cross-curricular lessons supporting and embracing Iñupiaq values, culture, history, language, and worldview as a foundation for instructional support in all content areas.

Community involvement & insight

I participated alongside community members in community events including holiday events and subsistence hunting.

Community insight

I regularly invited elders and families to share experiences and insights. Because of my interest and involvement, I was invited to participate with students from across the state in indigenous culture events including the Alaska Federation of Natives Youth & Elders conference.

Who's out there

I also sought opportunities to speak with authors like Bill Nye and David Blatner through social media. They answered questions and hosted activities like a live video "Pi Day" contest with other classrooms around the country. We even received some free books!

Social networking

Those eager to share experiences with their families were able to do so through social networking pages. It quickly became active, and through this, we enjoyed positive discussions more frequently than conferencing otherwise allowed.

Write Club

For six years I sponsored Write Club, an after-school program where growing writers of every interest and skill level could meet, share, workshop, and grow their personal and academic projects. We did everything from timed “write sprints” to collaborative writing and editing to random-prompt writing games (roll the dice!). National Novel Writing Month was the big event every November, and we also held poetry slams.

We also organized author talks, so students could hear straight from working writers and ask their own questions. We held Q&As with authors like Farrah Penn and David Blatner, among others, over Zoom.

The club grew so large that other teachers pitched in after hours. Students came back year after year, and some even returned to visit and help out when they were home from college.

To feed all that writing, I wrote the grant for a Creative Writing Resource Library: more than a hundred books, chosen by student vote, supporting both academic and personal writing. They became curriculum extension materials in class and the go-to shelf for Write Club, full of tips, tricks, and inspiration for writing characters, building worlds, and more.

Volunteering

North Texas Teen Book Festival (2017 to present)

Every year since 2017 I've volunteered at the North Texas Teen Book Festival, a free, one-day celebration of middle grade and young adult books at the Irving Convention Center. It started in 2015 expecting a few hundred readers; now it draws thousands of teens, families, librarians, and teachers to meet around 75 authors for panels and signings. I help run author events, keep lines and sessions moving, and track audience engagement, and I've had the fun of assisting authors like Marie Lu, Tahereh Mafi, Ransom Riggs, and Nnedi Okorafor. As a longtime English teacher and Write Club sponsor, there's nothing quite like watching a teenager meet the writer who made them love reading. (One of the festival's organizers has a few kind words about it, too.)

From 2017 to 2023 I also spent the Friday before each festival at Educator Day, a free professional day for teachers and librarians. Authors share their newest books in keynotes, panels, and conversations, and education specialists weigh in on what's happening in classrooms and libraries. The lineup stays a secret until you walk in, which is half the fun. I went as a teacher to preview the festival, and came back with experiences, ideas, and most of all, a stack of new books for my classroom shelves.
