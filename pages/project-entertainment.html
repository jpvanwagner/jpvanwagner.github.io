Theater is where I first learned to hold a room. From 2007 to 2011 I performed with a community Shakespeare company in Northern Michigan and in a string of college productions, everything from classic tragedy and American drama to a choral piece and something called Pancakes: The Musical.

With the Riverside Shakespeare Company I acted in Romeo and Juliet and A Midsummer Night's Dream. Shakespeare is a great teacher of the basics: you can't get through those lines without breath control, clear diction, and knowing exactly what you mean by every word, and an audience lets you know right away when you don't.

In college I joined the casts of One Flew Over the Cuckoo's Nest, Macbeth, The Crucible, The Holocaust Cantata, Pancakes: The Musical, and Convicts. Months of rehearsals taught me to memorize quickly, take notes gracefully, adjust on the fly, and trust the people around me.

In 2009 I stepped to the other side of the stage as a teacher's aide and assistant director for a college play production class's Much Ado About Nothing, coaching students on memorization and acting technique and helping put together the course materials. It was one of my first tastes of teaching. The same skills (projection, pacing, reading the room, and staying calm when something goes sideways) are the ones I use in front of every training group today.
